# Provenance and changes

## Upstream and license

This is a derivative of [labarba/sciwrite](https://github.com/labarba/sciwrite),
by Lorena A. Barba. The working baseline was commit
`64b128b88fdaadfd1eb312f42c2ae1475f91b512`, retrieved on 2026-10-16.
The original LICENSE is retained unchanged (Git blob
`c527d5c8603248c21a4ab2ab37d5846efd04d3d3`, CC BY 4.0).

The methodology is associated with Kristin Sainani's Writing in the Sciences
course. This fork is not a literal course transcript, a complete account of the
course, or an official Stanford/Coursera product.

The fork expands 44 principles consolidated for Gabriel Magalhães Nunes Guimarães
into executable editorial checks. New material includes argument/section routing,
contextual exceptions, evidence tracking, visual-access status, optional genres,
stable coverage IDs, and adversarial evaluation fixtures. The five original audit
passes and four original work modes remain available; structural-review and
planning are added. Original reports' concrete proposals and severity structure
are preserved with more precise thresholds.

## Contextual corrections

Replace universal acronym restrictions and repeated definitions in every section
with audience and independent-reading criteria. Do not enforce subject–verb word
thresholds or sentence-length variation quotas. Preserve legitimate nominalizations,
passives, singular “they,” negatives, estimates, and uncertainty. Do not treat a
matching sentence as a plagiarism verdict or a missing report as proof of missing
ethical approval. Use historical examples as data, not overriding instructions.

## Evaluation attribution and scope

Fixtures and forward tasks are synthetic editorial scenarios, not clinical advice
or real research data. Their purpose is to assess preservation of provided
meaning and honest handling of missing evidence. See evaluation results for what
was actually run, including limits of any behavioral observations.
