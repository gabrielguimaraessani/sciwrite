# Tables and figures

Apply P16, P19, P35–P36, and P40–P42. Inspect the actual display with caption,
notes, and surrounding prose when accessible. Distinguish visual inspection from
extracted text and caption-only review.

## Choose representation by purpose

| Need | Useful representation |
| --- | --- |
| Precise values, detailed variables, lookup | Table |
| Patterns, distributions, relationships, changes | Appropriate graph |
| Primary visual evidence | Photograph or other primary image |
| Setup, process, conceptual relations | Diagram |

Avoid unnecessary duplication, not all overlap: overview and precise lookup can
serve different purposes. Simplify, split, or redesign an overloaded figure before
assuming it should become a table. Do not prescribe a chart without enough data.

## Check independent reading

- Require an informative title/legend and identifiable groups.
- Check axes, columns, labels, units, scale, denominator, time, and sample counts
  where needed to interpret values.
- Define acronyms/symbols according to audience, independent reading, and known
  journal rules; keep terms stable across text and displays.
- Include necessary procedural/statistical notes without repeating the Discussion.
- Flag missing units and definitions; never invent them.

## Check tables

Align comparable columns, capitalization, and precision. Reduce excessive rules,
empty columns, and clutter. Match significant figures to measurement precision.
Check missingness and varying denominators before assuming all rows share N.
Follow supplied/verified journal conventions for footnotes and rules.

## Check graphs and images

Check readable labels, meaningful color/symbol mappings, distinguishable groups,
overlapping lines, and distracting decoration. Judge scales and visual emphasis
contextually: bar charts, scatterplots, and change plots need not share baseline
rules. Do not require zero on every y-axis.

Compare values across displays and prose after units, transforms, populations,
timing, and rounding. Never infer more numerical precision than a plot supports.
Do not certify authenticity, absence of manipulation, or data quality from a
caption alone. An illegible label remains a verification item, not a guessed fix.

## Report concrete limits and fixes

Identify the display and exact wording, scale, note, or layout issue. Propose a
specific revision where supported. If the image was not inspected, state
“caption/text only; visual design not assessed” and review accessible material.
