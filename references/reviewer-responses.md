# Reviewer responses and editorial review reports

Apply P02, P04–P07, P10, P14–P18, P25, P32, and P38. Distinguish editorial help
from technical peer review of methods or statistical validity.

## Respond point by point

| Comment | Decision | Response | Actual change/location | State |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identifiable reviewer wording | Accept, partly accept, or disagree with reason | Specific and respectful answer | Verified location or proposed move | Proposed / applied and verified / author input needed |

Address every supplied comment. Do not invent comments, experiments, analyses,
or action completion. Use “we have revised” only when revised wording or files
confirm it. If planning changes, label proposed responses as conditional on making
them. Never fabricate a line number or an additional analysis to satisfy a reviewer.

Support disagreement with available evidence and address the concern even when
declining its suggested solution. Keep tone noncombative and specific without
mandatory flattery. Cross-reference repeated issues while answering each comment.
Review response and manuscript together when both are supplied: polished wording
does not prove the promised changes occurred.

## Editorial peer-review reports

Identify the question and principal editorial issues briefly, then number concrete
revisions. Discuss the work, not the authors. Separate reporting gaps, internal
inconsistency, and issues needing technical review. Do not decide scientific
validity from prose alone. Follow supplied journal policy for editor-only versus
author-facing recommendations rather than inventing a universal format.
