# Grants, specific aims, and academic recommendations

Apply common clarity, structure, and evidence principles only for relevant tasks.

## Grants and specific aims

Extract actual funder criteria, prompt, format, limits, project facts, preliminary
evidence, and contribution. Align gap → objective → aims → approach → feasibility
→ expected payoff. Never invent preliminary data, resources, budgets, institutional
support, approvals, or impact estimates. Use a working hypothesis where justified,
not as a universal requirement. Distinguish expected outcomes from observed findings.

Explain importance, goal, each aim, and supported contribution succinctly. Keep
aims, plan, and supporting sections consistent. Flag feasibility questions without
pretending an editorial review establishes merit or eligibility. When planning is
requested, offer manageable steps without inventing deadlines or submitting an
application. Verify current sponsor rules when the task depends on them.

## Recommendation letters

Extract writer–candidate relationship, opportunity, observations, evidence, and
true support. Distinguish direct knowledge from attributed evidence. Calibrate
praise; do not manufacture rankings, superlatives, achievements, or willingness
to endorse. State recommendation and relationship, support relevant qualities with
examples, and close consistently. Avoid a CV recital or excessive focus on the writer.

Use requested format and provided submission instructions. Never invent letterhead,
signatures, contact details, or eligibility. If information is thin, offer a bounded
draft or ask for essential facts. Do not universalize course advice about who may
provide a draft or disclosure requirements without checking applicable rules.
Do not send letters or contact recipients without independent authorization.
