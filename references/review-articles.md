# Review articles

Apply P02–P07, P09–P19, P25, P32, P36, and P38–P42. Distinguish narrative review,
systematic review, and supplied meta-analysis. Never relabel narrative synthesis
to imply methods that were not performed.

## Synthesize rather than recite

Define a focused question, thesis, scope, and audience. Organize by themes,
methods, consensus, questions, and controversies rather than paper by paper.
Separate known, uncertain, contested, and unsupported claims. Compare populations,
methods, and outcomes before calling studies contradictory or directly comparable.

Make a working outline before rewriting. Use supplied or actually verified
citations; organization does not authorize fabricated references or an unstated
search. Do not claim comprehensive coverage without demonstrated search scope.

## Respect supplied review methods

For systematic reviews, check reporting of search, eligibility, selection,
extraction, appraisal, and synthesis when supplied. Missing reporting is not proof
of missing procedures. Never invent databases, dates, screening, risk-of-bias
assessments, protocols, or pooled estimates. Technical validity and formal guideline
compliance require a separately supported assessment when requested.

## Structure

State scope and purpose in Introduction, organize a justified body, then synthesize
support and gaps in the conclusion. Update the abstract after the review settles
when full text is available. Use displays for useful evidence comparisons without
pretending heterogeneous studies form a uniform dataset. Keep recommendations
proportionate to reviewed evidence.
