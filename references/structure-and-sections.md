# Structure and section checks

Apply P06–P14 and P31–P39 to the genre. Identify original research, protocol,
editorial, or another format before requiring empirical-study sections.

## Structural review and planning

1. Extract question, objective, and main message. Mark inferred messages as
   provisional. Identify supporting facts and essential missing evidence.
2. Make a reverse outline: one purpose per paragraph. Detect repetition,
   unsupported jumps, misplaced detail, and ideas needing separate paragraphs.
3. Propose specific moves, combinations, or cuts using real openings or known
   file/line locations. Do not invent locations or facts to bridge a gap.
4. Check central ideas, relevant support, logical order, useful openings/endings,
   and necessary transitions. Fix logic before adding signposting.
5. In planning mode, outline what supplied facts support and list gaps separately.
   Offer manageable steps and optional word targets; do not schedule commitments.

Displays → Results → Methods → Introduction → Discussion → Abstract is a useful
empirical-paper writing strategy, not a required order. Draft only when requested.
Reading aloud and outside feedback may reveal awkwardness; never claim either
occurred unless it did. Resolve structural changes before sentence polish.

## Introduction

- Focus context on the question, not a textbook overview of the discipline.
- Synthesize relevant knowledge; identify the actual gap or unresolved question.
- State aim, question, or hypothesis explicitly and explain the broad approach
  when useful without manufacturing novelty.
- Keep it focused; two to five paragraphs are a possible pattern, not a limit.
- Reserve extensive procedures and own-study interpretation for the appropriate
  section where the genre requires that division.

## Methods

- Check reporting of design, setting, participants/materials, eligibility,
  procedures, measures, timing, and analysis as relevant.
- Preserve doses, concentrations, temperature, pH, instruments, and conditions
  needed for interpretation or replication.
- Cite established procedures while explaining adaptations. Use headings,
  tables, or flow diagrams where clearer.
- Check applicable approval/consent reporting. A missing statement is not proof
  that approval was absent. Never invent committee names, numbers, or procedures.
- Use past tense for completed work and retain useful passive constructions.
- Evaluate descriptive completeness, not technical validity or actual
  reproducibility without separately authorized evidence-based assessment.

## Results

- Lead with the finding or comparison, then the supporting display.
- Select informative numbers with units and denominators. Do not repeat each
  table cell or select only favorable outcomes.
- Include relevant negative, control, and inconclusive findings. Nonsignificance
  is not evidence of no effect or equivalence.
- Use informative subsections for long Results.
- Keep procedural rationale and interpretation elsewhere unless the format
  combines them. Use past for findings and present for what a display shows.
- Clarify ambiguous “significant”: statistical and substantive importance differ.
  Do not add an analysis merely to change that word.

## Discussion and conclusion

- Answer the question first using the supplied findings.
- Explain meaning, relevant secondary results, and literature context.
- Distinguish possible mechanisms from demonstrated ones; verify claimed novelty
  rather than assuming it.
- Address meaningful strengths, limitations, and consequences for generalization,
  not a generic limitations paragraph.
- Identify open questions and proportionate future directions.
- Close with a supported message tied to the objective. A strong ending does not
  require greater certainty or repetition of the entire abstract.

## Abstract

- Write/update from the completed paper when available.
- Include context, objective, essential methods, principal results with decisive
  numbers, and an answer supported by those results.
- Make it independent; define acronyms where required.
- Follow supplied or actually verified word limits and structured/unstructured
  format. Otherwise use general conventions without inventing journal rules.
- If it is the only supplied text, do not claim consistency with unseen sections.

## Other formats

Describe protocol procedures as planned, not completed. Review an editorial for
thesis, evidence, reasoning, and implications without requiring participants or
empirical Results. Load the review module for reviews. Respect combined sections
instead of splitting solely to satisfy a generic IMRaD pattern.
