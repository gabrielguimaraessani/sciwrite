# Broader audiences

Apply P01–P05, P09–P19, P25, P29, P36, P38, and P43. Explain evidence to intelligent
nonspecialists without substituting drama for significance.

## Lay summaries and media explanation

Lead with message and relevance. Explain unfamiliar terms step by step with
concrete examples, ordinary units, and relevant detail. Anticipate misunderstandings
and put limitations where readers need them. Use stories only when supported;
never invent quotations or patient experiences.

## Numbers and risk

Preserve population, denominator, period, comparator, and uncertainty. Distinguish
relative and absolute change. Add absolute context only when supported; relative
risk alone does not identify an absolute difference. Natural frequencies can help
when supported and authorized, but do not invent baselines or perform an unstated
analysis. Do not choose a dramatic metric that obscures practical magnitude.
This is editorial checking, not clinical guidance or statistical analysis.

## News and interviews

For requested news-style writing, lead with supported news, add essential context,
then findings, caveats, and perspective. Use only provided or verified quotations.
A closing quote or return to the opening is optional. For interview preparation,
develop accurate messages and likely misunderstandings without claiming an
interview occurred.
